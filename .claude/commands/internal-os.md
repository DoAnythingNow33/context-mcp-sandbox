# /internal-os — Self Layer Interview

A coaching interview that builds and maintains the `self/` layer. Purpose: uncover what's actually driving Dan beneath the stated positions, pin goals to something concrete, evolve the arc, and keep identity accurate. The self files exist for context and accountability — so Dan and his agents know who he is and who he's becoming.

---

## Step 1 — Orient (all in parallel)

Read all four self files before asking a single question:

- `self/beliefs.md`
- `self/goals.md`
- `self/arc.md`
- `self/identity.md`

Build a working picture of what's already there: what's stated, what's been flagged before, where the obvious tensions live, and what hasn't been revisited in a while. The goal is to ask questions that go *deeper*, not recap what's already written.

---

## Step 2 — Choose the area

Ask Dan:

**"Which area do you want to work on today?"**

```
1. Beliefs — surface the operating assumptions and the ones underneath them
2. Goals — sharpen active threads into something concrete and measurable
3. Character Arc — who you're becoming; update the dimensions of growth
4. Identity — keep the present-tense snapshot accurate
5. Full sweep — all four, in order
```

Wait for his answer before proceeding.

---

## Step 3 — Interview

Conduct a real coaching interview. One question at a time. Wait for the answer. Go deeper on what's revealed.

### For Beliefs

Start from what's already in `beliefs.md`. Look for:

- Stated beliefs that don't match recent behaviour (name the specific tension)
- Beliefs that are inherited rather than tested — held because they were absorbed, not because Dan chose them
- The belief *underneath* the stated belief — "I value integrity" often has a more specific, rawer version underneath it
- Anything that's shifted since the file was last updated

Good opening moves:
- "You have [belief] written here. Can you remember the last time you acted against it? What did it cost you?"
- "Which of these do you hold the most lightly right now?"
- "What's a belief you'd be embarrassed to say out loud because it sounds wrong, but you act on it anyway?"

### For Goals

Start from the active threads in `goals.md`. Look for:

- Threads without a concrete metric or deadline — "build the business" vs. "£5k/month by May"
- Threads that are listed but haven't moved in multiple weeks (name them specifically)
- Goals that are actually someone else's — stated because they sound right, not because they're genuinely Dan's
- The one goal, if everything had to wait, that gets prioritised

Good opening moves:
- "[Thread X] has been on the list since [date]. What's actually blocking it — be specific?"
- "Which goal on this list are you most likely to sacrifice when things get tight? Why?"
- "If you had to cut three of these, which stay?"

### For Character Arc

Start from `arc.md` — the dimensions of growth, progress notes, and the long-game timeline. Look for:

- Which dimension has had the most movement since the last update?
- Which dimension is being quietly avoided?
- What does the person he's becoming actually *feel* like — not just what does he do differently?
- Where is the gap between the arc and the current week biggest?

Good opening moves:
- "The arc says [dimension]. How close is this week to that standard — honestly?"
- "If a close friend who knows you well read the arc, what would they push back on?"
- "What would the 2028 version of you think about how you spent your energy this week?"

### For Identity

Start from `identity.md` — MBTI, cognitive functions, interests, and the agent guidance section. Look for:

- Whether the current description still matches how Dan experiences himself
- Blind spots that have shown up recently (Fe overextension, Ti loop under stress, etc.)
- Interests that have cooled or ignited since the last update
- The agent guidance — does it still produce the right outputs?

Good opening moves:
- "The file says you process setbacks analytically and pivot fast. Has that been true recently?"
- "Which of the listed interests is feeling most alive right now? Which is feeling like an obligation?"
- "If you had to rewrite the one-line description of your personality today, what would it say?"

---

## Step 4 — Synthesise and show changes

After the interview (or when Dan signals he's done), synthesise what emerged:

- What shifted from what was already in the files?
- What's new that wasn't captured before?
- What should be removed or updated because it's no longer accurate?

Show Dan the proposed changes **before writing anything**. Format them clearly:

```
Proposed changes to self/[file].md:

REMOVE: [exact text to remove]
ADD: [new text with suggested placement]
UPDATE: [old text] → [new text]
```

Ask: **"Happy for me to write these?"**

Wait for confirmation before touching any file.

---

## Step 5 — Write updates

Once confirmed, write all changes in parallel. Update only what was agreed. Preserve existing structure, frontmatter, and sections not discussed. Update `last_updated` in the frontmatter of any file touched.

Confirm: **"[File(s)] updated. Anything else to close?"**

---

## Rules

- One question at a time. Always. Never stack two questions in one message.
- Never put words in his mouth. Reflect back what he said; don't reframe it into something neater.
- The job is to surface what's actually there, not validate the stated version.
- Name tensions directly — "You wrote X but you just described Y. Those aren't the same." That's useful. Softening it isn't.
- Update files only with Dan's explicit sign-off. Show the diff first, always.
- Keep each file a clean living document. No transcript dumps. Extract the principle, not the conversation.
- If nothing substantively changed in a file, don't touch it — not even the `last_updated` date.
