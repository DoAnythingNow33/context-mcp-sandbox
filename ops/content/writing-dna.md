# Writing DNA — Daniel Tejada Salazar

## Voice

- Raw, honest, personal. Real events and real emotions — not polished narratives.
- Philosophical but always grounded in lived experience. The concept earns its place by connecting to something real.
- Conviction without arrogance. He knows what he knows because he's lived it.
- Vulnerable as a strength — shares depression, anxiety, confusion, addiction as part of the story, not as disclaimers.
- Spiritual undertone. Not preachy. Present as atmosphere.
- Direct. No filler. No corporate speak. Nothing that sounds like it came from a template.

## Sentence Structure

- Short sentences. Often fragments. One idea per line.
- Builds pressure through repetition then releases it.
- Uses dashes and ellipses for pause and rhythm — not for decoration.
- Bold used for emphasis on the exact word that carries the weight.
- Lists that breathe: "free, expansive, held, loved, and limitless" — the last word always lands hardest.

## Structural Pattern (Substack)

1. Opens with a scene, a week, a personal moment — not a thesis
2. Moves into a concept or reframe that emerged from it
3. Names the old pattern / what was wrong
4. Lands on a principle, a decision, or a conviction
5. Closes with a line that feels earned, not declared

## Structural Pattern (LinkedIn — adapted)

1. Hook: one short line that names the tension or contradiction
2. Setup: 2–4 short lines establishing personal context
3. The turn: the thing that was caught, revealed, or shifted
4. The principle: what this means — said plainly, one line
5. Close: the contrast between most people and the alternative — leaves the reader somewhere

## Recurring Themes

- Identity as something you build, not inherit
- Decisions as the unit of change ("the decisions I have made have led me here")
- AI as a system, not a tool
- Conviction vs. noise — tuning out external opinions, cementing in your own experience
- The tension between vision and reality — naming it clearly rather than resolving it too fast
- Fatherhood as a mirror ("what will my son see when he looks at me")
- Business and spirituality woven together naturally — not separate lanes

## What He Never Does

- Doesn't start with "Today I want to talk about..."
- Doesn't soften hard truths with qualifiers
- Doesn't end on a question (he answers his own questions)
- Doesn't use buzzwords or hustle-culture language
- Doesn't explain the metaphor — he trusts the reader to land it
- Doesn't write long paragraphs — white space is part of the voice

## Signature Moves

- Names the contradiction before offering the insight
- Speaks to himself and the reader at the same time
- Turns a personal failure or gap into a principle — not a confession
- Uses "I decided and I did" energy — short, final, no explanation needed

## TED-Talk AI Slop: Editing Checklist

Six patterns that infect LLM-generated and engagement-baited writing. Strip all of them on review.

### 1. Contrastive Rhetorical Framing
**Tell:** "Not just X, but Y" — manufacturing false stakes.
**Example:** *"Amazon isn't just buying content. They're buying credibility."*
**Fix:** State the objective directly. *"Amazon is purchasing content to build its credibility."*

### 2. Staccato Rhetorical Q&A
**Tell:** Asking a basic question just to answer it with a punchy one-liner.
**Example:** *"What changed? The math did."*
**Fix:** Merge into one declarative sentence. *"The math changed."* Trust the reader.

### 3. The Unjustified Em-Dash Deluge
**Tell:** Em-dashes as dramatic pauses or choppy thought-splicing.
**Example:** *"The old model—hire more writers to chase more pageviews to sell more ads—is breaking down."*
**Fix:** Replace with commas, parentheses, or break into two clean sentences.

### 4. Compulsive Triplet Framing
**Tell:** Forcing ideas into groups of three for a rehearsed, chanting cadence.
**Example:** *"Not for advertising. Not for distribution. For AI training."*
**Fix:** Break the rhythm. Vary sentence structure. If it doesn't require three, cut the fluff.

### 5. The Inspirational Pivot (Fake Profundity)
**Tell:** Zooming from a specific topic out to humanity/the future/consciousness.
**Example:** *"This isn't just about AI. It's about humanity."*
**Fix:** Kill it entirely. Keep scope anchored to the actual subject.

### 6. Groundless Universal Authority
**Tell:** "Studies show," "experts agree," "research proves" — no source named.
**Fix:** Name the institution, author, and year. If you can't find it in two minutes, delete the stat.

### 7. Ghost Quotes and Hallucinated Attributions
**Tell:** Suspiciously clean quotes from Jobs/Musk/Churchill that fit too perfectly.
**Fix:** Cross-reference every quote. If exact context, date, and publisher can't be verified — rephrase as general sentiment, no attribution.
