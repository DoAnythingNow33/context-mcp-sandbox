Nobody trusts a tool they've never broken through something difficult with.

I've watched a lot of people "try AI."

They test it on easy things.
Get mediocre results.
Decide it's overhyped.

And they're right — on easy things, it's mediocre.

The trust forms somewhere else.

It forms when something breaks, you don't know why, you paste the error in anyway, follow the instruction — and it works.

Then it breaks again. You do it again.

Three walls later you're doing things you would have called technical six months ago.

That's when someone in a session said to me: **"You, me, and Claude."**

Trust in a tool — like trust in a person — is built through hard things done together.

Most people quit when AI doesn't work the first time.

That's the exact moment the learning was about to start.
