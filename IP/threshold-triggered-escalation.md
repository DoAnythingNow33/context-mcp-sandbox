# Threshold-Triggered Escalation

## What It Is

Escalation should be triggered by objective thresholds detected automatically, not by whoever shouts loudest. The well-designed escalation model has clear stages (flag → dip-check → strategy intervention → senior leadership) and the entry to each stage is triggered by measurable conditions, not by a client calling the founder directly.

## Why It Matters

Reactive escalation — where problems surface because someone made enough noise — systematically favours the loudest clients and the most visible problems. The quieter issue, the underperforming account that hasn't complained yet, doesn't get attention until it's a crisis. Threshold-triggered escalation fixes this by making detection objective and automatic. AI is well-suited to the detection layer; humans are responsible for the response.

## How to Use It

**As a coach:** When a founder is constantly firefighting, ask: how do you currently find out about problems? If the answer involves clients calling them directly, reactive escalation is the diagnosis.

**As a consultant:** Design the escalation model first (what are the stages, what triggers each), then build the automated detection that feeds into it. Don't automate before the model is clear.

**As a content creator:** You're not drowning in fires because you're unlucky — you're drowning because your system only tells you about problems after they've become emergencies.

## Content Angles

- Reactive escalation rewards the loudest client, not the most urgent problem.
- Automation doesn't create accountability — it makes sure accountability kicks in at the right moment.
- The goal isn't to remove humans from escalation; it's to remove arbitrary triggers.
