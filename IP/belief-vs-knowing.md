# Belief vs. Knowing

## What It Is

Beliefs update through experience that generates *knowing* — not through argument or intellectual understanding. When a lived experience produces a felt sense of truth that contradicts an existing belief, the knowing overrides the belief. Telling someone their belief is wrong does nothing. Creating the conditions for them to *experience* something different is what actually rewires.

## Why It Matters

Most coaching and therapy tries to address limiting beliefs at the cognitive level — "let's examine the evidence," "is this really true?" — but the belief wasn't installed cognitively. It was installed through experience, emotion, and repeated exposure. Arguing against it doesn't touch the part of the brain that's running it. What does touch it is a direct experience — a moment where the person *knows* something different through having felt it. The knowing lands at the same level the belief was installed. That's what allows it to override.

The distinction also explains why clients can intellectually agree with a reframe and then continue the old behaviour. The belief hasn't updated — the knowing hasn't arrived yet. The work isn't to repeat the argument more convincingly. It's to help them create the experience that generates the knowing.

## How to Use It

**As a coach:** When a client understands the reframe intellectually but isn't moving, don't push the reasoning harder. Ask: what experience would make this *real* for you? What would you need to feel — not know — to have this update? Then help them create conditions for that experience. Small, low-stakes, on a new path.

**As a consultant:** Relevant in change management. When a team "agrees" with a new direction but behaviour hasn't shifted, the belief hasn't updated. You need evidence they can *feel* — an early win on the new approach, not just a compelling deck.

**As a content creator:** The post writes itself: "You can't argue yourself out of a belief. You have to experience your way out." Frame with a personal story — when did you *know* something that changed a belief you'd held for years? It wasn't a conversation. It was a moment.

## Content Angles

- Your brain doesn't update beliefs on logic. It updates them on experience. Stop trying to convince yourself. Start trying to *show* yourself.
- The gap between "I understand this" and "I've changed" is exactly the size of the experience that hasn't happened yet.
- Argument prepares the ground. Experience plants the seed. Only one of those actually grows something.
