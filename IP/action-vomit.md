# Action Vomit

## What It Is
The impulse that rises before action — like bubbles rising to the surface of a drink, it has to come out. If suppressed (talked out of it, distracted, procrastinated), the feeling returns but weaker each time. Acting on the first, strongest hit is the unlock.

## Why It Matters
Most productivity advice focuses on systems and schedules. This concept targets something upstream: the body's own readiness signal. Suppressing that signal doesn't neutralize the urge — it just erodes the energy available to act on it. The first hit is always the strongest.

## How to Use It
**As a coach:** When a client describes a recurring impulse they keep not-acting on, name it. Ask: "What does the impulse feel like when it first arrives?" Then explore what happens in the gap between the impulse and the (non-)action. The goal is to shorten that gap.

**As a consultant:** Useful when diagnosing why teams don't execute on good ideas — the collective version of action vomit is killed in meetings, by consensus-seeking, by waiting for the "right" moment.

**As a content creator:** The post writes itself: the thing you keep meaning to do but never do is getting weaker every time you don't do it.

## Content Angles
