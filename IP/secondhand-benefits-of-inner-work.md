# Secondhand Benefits of Inner Work

## What It Is
When one person does sustained inner work, the benefits spill into their relationships — not just their own wellbeing. Partners, parents, children, and friends experience a different, regulated version of that person. The work is personal, but the effects are relational.

## Why It Matters
The dominant framing of inner work is selfish — something you do for yourself. This flips it: inner work is one of the highest-leverage things you can do for the people you love. It's an underrated argument that cuts through the guilt of prioritising personal development.

## How to Use It
**As a coach:** Use it when a client feels guilty about investing in themselves, especially if they're a caregiver or have people depending on them. The work they do shows up differently in every relationship they have.

**As a consultant:** Relevant when coaching leaders — when a leader regulates better, the entire team operates differently. The culture benefit of a leader doing inner work is real and measurable.

**As a content creator:** A post reframing personal development as relational generosity — the people around you are the accidental beneficiaries of your work.
