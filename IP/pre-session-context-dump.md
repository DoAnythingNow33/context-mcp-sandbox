# Pre-Session Context Dump

## What It Is

Before any discovery or extraction session, send the client a written list of what you need and ask them to share all relevant existing documentation — org charts, process maps, SOPs, WorkFlowy exports, anything that describes how the business or role works. You ingest this before the call, not on it.

## Why It Matters

Discovery sessions are expensive in time and attention. If the first 30 minutes are spent establishing basics that already exist in a document, you've wasted 30 minutes of extraction capacity — and the client's patience. Pre-session context dumps let you arrive already knowing the structure, vocabulary, and prior thinking. Sessions become about gaps, tensions, and nuance, not catch-up. The prep before the first session is half the session.

## How to Use It

**As a coach:** Ask new clients to send any existing journaling, self-reflection documents, or prior coaching notes before the first session. Arrive knowing their language. The first session goes deeper when you're not spending it building a baseline picture you could have read.

**As a consultant:** Make it standard onboarding. Send a written list, not a verbal ask — verbal asks produce partial responses, written lists produce complete ones. Give them a format so they don't have to guess what's relevant. This also signals professionalism: you've thought about what you need before walking in the door.

**As a content creator:** "The question I send every new client before our first call" — process content that demonstrates a systematic, respectful approach to discovery. Resonates with anyone who does client work.

## Content Angles

- If you're figuring out the basics on the call, you're not doing discovery — you're doing orientation. Those aren't the same thing.
- Send a written list of what you need. Verbal asks are easy to forget and easy to answer partially.
- The best sessions start before the session. The prep is half the work.
- Arriving having already read their docs signals something to a client: you take their time seriously.
