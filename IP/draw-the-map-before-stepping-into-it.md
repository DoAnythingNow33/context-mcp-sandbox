# Draw the Map Before Stepping Into It

## What It Is

Jamie's framing for the phase before building: spend the early sessions defining what the offer is and isn't, rather than jumping straight into building infrastructure. You draw the map of the territory first — what problems exist, what offers map to them, what the pricing and rationale are — before you commit time to walking through it.

## Why It Matters

The instinct with a new joint offer (or any new build) is to start producing artifacts — decks, demos, agents — because motion feels like progress. But building on an undefined map wastes the exact resource it's meant to save: time spent on infrastructure that may be solving the wrong problem. Naming the scoping phase explicitly ("we're drawing the map, not stepping into it yet") gives both people permission to stay in brainstorming and definition mode without the anxiety of feeling behind.

## How to Use It

**As a coach:** Use this with clients who feel pressure to "just start building" — give them permission to spend a session purely mapping the problem space before committing to an approach.

**As a consultant:** Use this to sequence a new engagement or joint offer: an explicit map-drawing phase (define the problem, the audience, the offers) before a build phase (demos, decks, pilots). Say it out loud to the other party so the slower pace reads as deliberate, not stalled.

**As a content creator:** A useful reframe against build-first bias in AI/agency work — most wasted infrastructure comes from skipping the map.

## Content Angles

- The instinct to start building is often anxiety dressed as productivity. Drawing the map first is the harder, more disciplined move.
- The question this answers: "How do we know we're building the right thing before we build anything?"
- Story: two people scoping a new offer resist the urge to build a demo in week one, and instead spend weeks defining the problem space — which changes what they build.
