# Pain-Anchored MVP Selection

## What It Is

When choosing which productised AI offer to lead with, pick the one that maps to a pain the prospect already has and already spends money working around — not the offer that's most technically impressive. Between "AI system" (novel, exciting, no attached need) and "Notion manager" (unglamorous, but solving a headache teams already pay someone to babysit), the second is the better MVP because the need pre-exists the pitch.

## Why It Matters

Novelty doesn't sell — recognition does. A prospect can nod along to "we build AI agents" and still do nothing, because there's no felt cost to inaction. A prospect who's already paying someone (or losing time) to keep a shared Notion workspace from rotting has a cost they can name, which makes the offer a fix rather than an experiment. The heuristic reverses the instinct to lead with the most sophisticated capability: lead with the shallowest gap between "what hurts" and "what we sell."

## How to Use It

**As a coach:** Use this with clients stuck choosing between multiple product ideas — ask "which of these already has someone paying for a worse version of the solution?" That's the one to build first.

**As a consultant:** Apply this when scoping a new productised offer (as with Jamie on agency services) — before building demos or decks, map ideas against pains prospects already name unprompted, and let that ranking decide the MVP, not technical interest.

**As a content creator:** A contrarian angle against "build the most impressive AI demo" — the best-selling AI product is often the boring one that fixes a known mess.

## Content Angles

- Most people pick their MVP by asking "what can we build," not "what already hurts." The order should be reversed.
- The question that exposes weak offers: "Is anyone already paying to solve a worse version of this problem?"
- Story: pitching "AI agents" gets polite interest; pitching "someone to keep your Notion from falling apart" gets a yes, because the second one names a cost the prospect already feels.
