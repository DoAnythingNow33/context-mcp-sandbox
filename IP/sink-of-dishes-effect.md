# The Sink of Dishes Effect

## What It Is
The perceived weight of your cognitive load — to-dos, ideas, worries, half-finished thoughts — always feels massive until you write it out. Once externalised, it's usually just a few concrete things: two plates and a bowl instead of a full sink. Externalisation doesn't solve the items; it makes them visible and manageable.

## Why It Matters
Overwhelm is mostly a perception problem, not a volume problem. The mind holds unexternalised items as a blob of dread. The moment they become a list, the blob dissolves and you can deal with individual items. This is why brain dumps work — not because they solve anything, but because they end the blurred experience of carrying everything at once.

## How to Use It
**As a coach:** Use it when a client says they're overwhelmed or have "so much going on." Ask them to empty the sink — do a brain dump, spoken or written. Then look at it together. The sink is rarely as full as it felt.

**As a consultant:** Useful in project scoping when a client feels like everything is on fire. Externalise the full list, separate the real fires from the perceived fires, and prioritise. The act of listing is often more relieving than the prioritisation itself.

**As a content creator:** A post about why you feel overwhelmed even when you don't actually have that much to do — and the one move that ends it.
