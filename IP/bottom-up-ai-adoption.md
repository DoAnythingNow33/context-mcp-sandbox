# Bottom-Up AI Adoption

## What It Is

Start AI implementation with the people doing the daily work — not with the founders' vision of how AI should work. Extract actual workflows first. Build tools that fit the real process. Only then validate against leadership's goals.

## Why It Matters

Top-down AI mandates fail because they start from an abstraction — a leader's idea of what the team does — rather than the ground truth of how work actually flows. The result is tools that solve imagined problems. Bottom-up extraction surfaces the real friction: where time is lost, what's repeated, what context is held in someone's head that dies when they leave. Once you have that, you can build AI that actually helps.

This is also why founders-first (rather than whole-team-first) is the right Phase 1 scope for most consulting engagements. Disha Bhatnagar's pushback in the Growify call was exactly right: get the founders to 70–80% clarity on which problems to solve before asking the team to adopt anything.

## How to Use It

**As a coach:** The same principle applies to personal change. Don't start with the vision — start with how the person actually spends their time. The gap between intention and actual behaviour is where the work is.

**As a consultant:** Framing the ontology sessions as extraction (not training) is how you sell this. "We're not telling your team how to use AI. We're learning how your team actually works." That framing removes resistance.

**As a content creator:** "Why most AI rollouts fail at the point they touch the team" — the post writes itself. The counter-intuitive move: start with the people doing the work, not the people with the vision.

## Content Angles

- Most companies try to install AI from the top. The ones that succeed built it from the ground.
- A founder's vision of how their team works is almost never accurate. That's not criticism — it's structural.
- The question isn't "how do we use AI?" It's "what does the work actually look like, and where does it break?"
