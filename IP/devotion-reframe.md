# The Devotion Reframe

## What It Is

Replacing the word "work" — or any label that has accumulated resistance, shame, or obligation — with a personally-defined concept of devotion that ties daily actions to life purpose. The actions don't change; the meaning does. That meaning change is what removes the block.

## Why It Matters

Most people try to overcome resistance to work through discipline, systems, or motivation. But when the *word itself* has been coded as a path to self-sabotage or shame, the brain routes away from it before any discipline can fire. Changing the label changes the routing. "Devotion" isn't a rebranding trick — it's a complete recategorisation: from obligation to expression, from chore to act of worship. Once the header changes, anything filed under it inherits the new energy.

## How to Use It

**As a coach:** When a client has full knowledge of what to do but can't get started, look at what the work *means* to them — not what it is. Ask them to name the feeling that comes up when they think about doing it. If the feeling is resistance, shame, or pressure, the label is the problem. Help them define a new container — their version of devotion — that ties the same actions to something they genuinely care about.

**As a consultant:** Less directly applicable in consulting engagements, but surfaces when a team has "initiative fatigue" — when the label on a project has accumulated failure associations and people route around it. Renaming and reframing the purpose (not just the project) can unstick a stuck team.

**As a content creator:** This is a post. The insight that the word you use changes whether you do the thing is immediately relatable — every creator has a version of this. Frame it as a question: what word are you using for your work, and is it helping or blocking you?
