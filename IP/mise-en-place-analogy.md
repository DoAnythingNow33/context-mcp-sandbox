# The Mise en Place Analogy

## What It Is

Mise en place (French: "everything in its place") is the chef's discipline of preparing all ingredients before cooking begins. Nothing happens at the stove until the prep is done. The analogy to AI: most people use AI like a chef who starts cooking without any prep — improvised, reactive, ingredients everywhere. The context folder is the mise en place. Once done, execution is fast, clean, and repeatable.

## Why It Matters

The analogy does two things at once: it normalises the upfront investment (nobody argues that prep work is wasteful) and it reframes the frustration people have with AI (inconsistent outputs, hallucinations, unhelpful responses) as a prep problem, not a tool problem. It also implies mastery — a great chef is distinguished by their prep, not just their technique at the stove.

## How to Use It

**As a coach:** The analogy maps cleanly to personal change work. Most people try to change behaviour (the cooking) without doing the inner prep (understanding who they are, what's actually blocking them, what they want). The sessions are the mise en place.

**As a consultant:** Use it to frame why the ontology sessions are necessary before any AI tooling is built. "We're not building anything yet. We're doing mise en place." It removes impatience — no one argues with prep.

**As a content creator:** "Most people use AI like a chef who starts cooking without doing any prep." That first line stops the scroll. The rest writes itself.

## Content Angles

- You don't blame the pan when the food is bad. You look at the prep.
- AI without context is a kitchen with no prep done. You can still cook — it'll just be chaotic.
- The chefs who produce consistently great food aren't faster at the stove. They're better at preparation.
