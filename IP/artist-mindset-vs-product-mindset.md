## What It Is

A founder failure pattern: someone evaluates a business decision through the lens of personal taste or creative control ("do I like this, does this fit what I made it to be") instead of through whether it moves the product or company forward. The tell is a founder rejecting a viable commercial opportunity for reasons that sound like curation, not strategy.

## Why It Matters

Founders who stay in "artist mode" past the point where their company needs to operate as a business will keep losing opportunities that look irrational from the outside — because the real filter isn't commercial logic, it's ego and taste. This is a reliable diagnostic: when a founder's stated reason for passing on something doesn't hold up commercially, look for a taste/control objection underneath it. It predicts how the founder will behave next time, and it's a signal of whether they're serious about growing past their current size.

## How to Use It

- **As a coach:** Helps a founder client see when they're protecting their taste rather than their business — a concrete, low-ego-threat way to name the pattern before it costs them a relationship or a deal.
- **As a consultant:** A read on founder maturity and investability during discovery — if the founder is filtering opportunities through "do I like it" rather than "does it work," expect resistance to market data, comps, and outside input generally, not just on this one decision.
- **As a content creator:** A sharp, relatable founder-psychology post — most people think they're being commercially rigorous when they're actually protecting their taste.
