# Voting for Timelines

## What It Is
A decisive action is a vote for a specific timeline. When you take the action, doors that were already aligned with that direction open. Hesitation or avoidance casts a vote for the other timeline — not for neutrality, but for a different trajectory.

## Why It Matters
Framing decisions as votes reframes indecision: there is no neutral. Doing nothing is still a vote. This makes the cost of hesitation concrete and cuts through the illusion that "waiting to decide" preserves optionality.

## How to Use It
**As a coach:** Use it when a client is stuck in analysis paralysis or waiting for certainty before acting. "What timeline are you voting for right now?" makes the implicit consequence of inaction explicit without being confrontational.

**As a consultant:** Useful for organisations delaying strategic commitments — the "let's wait and see" posture is a vote too, and usually for the less interesting timeline.

**As a content creator:** Anchor the post in a specific decision the reader is deferring and show what timeline they're actually voting for by not deciding.
