# Context Fragmentation

## What It Is

When every team member runs their own AI account with no shared context, the organisation's AI outputs become inconsistent — not because the AI is bad, but because each instance is operating blind to the others. The root failure mode isn't capability; it's that institutional knowledge is scattered across dozens of isolated sessions.

## Why It Matters

Most AI adoption fails at the team level for this reason. Companies spend money on AI tools and get disappointing, inconsistent results, then blame the technology. The actual problem is structural: the shared context that would make the AI powerful never gets built. One shared account plus one brand context folder per client changes the output quality more than switching to a better model.

## How to Use It

**As a coach:** When a client says "AI doesn't really work for my team," ask to see their setup. If everyone has their own account and there's no shared brand context, that's the diagnosis.

**As a consultant:** The audit question is: where does your brand knowledge live, and which AI sessions can access it? The fix is architectural — shared account, structured context folders, consistent templates — not a capability upgrade.

**As a content creator:** The post that reframes disappointing AI results from "the tool is limited" to "the context was never built."

## Content Angles

- AI is only as good as the context it has access to — and most teams give it none.
- The real AI investment isn't the subscription fee; it's building the shared context that makes the tool usable.
- Every team member using their own AI account is like every chef working from a different recipe — the food will never taste the same.
